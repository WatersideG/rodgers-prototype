# UI Patterns

The pattern evidence base is the Flying Bridge UI & UX Research Report (August 2026,
274 sources). It is the deepest pattern research Waterside holds. It was written for
one property, so it carries a scope caveat, noted at the end.

Findings the report puts first `[documented]`:

- Speed is a luxury attribute. In the Google and Deloitte study of 37 brand sites and
  30M+ sessions, luxury visitors were the most speed-sensitive vertical measured: a
  0.1-second improvement produced a 40.1% lift in product-page-to-basket progression
  and 20.6% more contact-page visits. Every visual decision is subordinate to the
  performance budget.
- High-consideration buyers research anonymously. Only 21% of boat buyers submitted a
  documented lead before purchase, and 85% bought a different boat than the one they
  first inquired about. Sites must serve long anonymous sessions, not only conversion
  paths.

Consult these for anything the Flying Bridge report does not cover.

- Nielsen Norman Group — ten usability heuristics, plus free reports and articles.
  `[documented]` as a designated source of truth.
- Baymard Institute — premium research library. Account-gated; see
  `sources-and-refresh.md`.

- Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Google Material Design, web.dev, W3C/WCAG.

  `design-standard.md`, section 5.

## Scope caveat

The Flying Bridge research is written for a tri-business marine property: dealership,
marina, restaurant. Its findings on orientation, multi-location structure and
high-consideration purchase behavior generalize well to South Peak, Longfellow and
Tides. Its vertical-specific findings do not automatically transfer to a restaurant or
retail brand.

Whether the report gets generalized into a portfolio-wide pattern standard, or stays a
cited reference, is `[open]`.
